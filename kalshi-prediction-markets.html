The 99-Cent Question: Kalshi Prediction Markets

This STATS 414 midterm project, completed with Juan Cortes-Aguiar and Ravikiran Madichetty, asked how accurate Kalshi's prices are. Kalshi lets people trade yes/no contracts that pay $1 or $0, so a contract's price works as the crowd's probability. We analyzed every hourly candle in the course Kalshi database from Oct 8 to 27, 2026 (7.17 million rows across 839,613 markets) to see whether prices are calibrated and whether favorites priced at 90 to 99 cents are as safe as they look.

The exploratory work showed that sports and crypto dominate the platform, with sports at 76% of contracts traded and the top 1% of markets carrying 82% of volume. Prices were calibrated within half a point on average across 129,866 markets, with a favorite and long-shot bias at the extremes. Among 10,351 favorites (513 of which lost), Economics contracts, mostly AAA gas-price markets, lost 0.5% of the time versus 3.5% implied by price, a gap that held in every period.

We then split the favorites by date into train, validation, and test sets and tuned elastic-net logistic regression, random forest, and XGBoost models, each with and without volume, against the quoted price as a baseline. No model beat the price: it had the best test Brier score (0.04624), and volume added nothing. Buying every test favorite at the ask still lost $19.61 on 2,299 contracts, even though 95.0% of them won, before fees.

The raw Kalshi data is course material and is not published here, so this page shows the analysis and the presentation only.
