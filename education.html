Coursework and Learning Areas

Relevant UCLA undergraduate and graduate coursework, organized by the technical areas each class strengthened.

Probability, Regression, and Statistical Modeling

Core theory and modeling courses that strengthened my foundation for inference, prediction, diagnostics, optimization, simulation, and graduate-level statistical work.

  • MATH 32A / 32B - Calculus of Several Variables: Multivariable differentiation, vector fields, multiple integration, and integration over curves and surfaces.
  • MATH 33A - Linear Algebra and Applications: Systems of equations, matrix methods, bases, dimension, least-squares approximation, eigenvalues, diagonalization, and singular value decomposition.
  • STATS 100A - Introduction to Probability: Probability theory and applications, including random variables, distributions, expectation, variance, joint distributions, and limit theorems.
  • STATS 100B - Introduction to Mathematical Statistics: Estimation, properties of estimators, confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, likelihood methods, and classical inference.
  • STATS 100C - Linear Models: Matrix-based linear regression, model fitting, general linear hypothesis testing, inference, residual analysis, and principal component regression.
  • STATS 101A - Introduction to Data Analysis and Regression: Applied regression with general linear and generalized linear models, including diagnostics, graphical procedures, and bootstrapping.
  • STATS 400 - Introduction to Probability Modeling: Probability theory, probability models, stochastic processes, and real applications.
  • STATS 402 - Applied Regression: Linear models for understanding systems and predicting outcomes, including inference, least-squares estimates, intervals, model building, diagnostics, and bootstrapping.
Experiments, Consulting, and Statistical Communication

Courses focused on experiment-driven decisions, stakeholder communication, and translating statistical work into useful recommendations.

  • STATS 101B - Introduction to Design and Analysis of Experiments: Experimental design fundamentals, including randomization, blocking, ANOVA, multiple comparisons, power, sample size, and block designs.
  • STATS 140XP / 141XP - Practice of Statistical Consulting: Two-term consulting sequence for real-world data projects, including framing statistical questions, modeling, analysis, client communication, and final presentations.
  • STATS 419 - Experimental Design: Randomization, blocking, treatment comparisons, factorial designs, Latin squares, fractional factorial designs, and response surfaces.
  • STATS 421 - Advanced Statistical Communication: Verbal and written communication for statistics in workplace settings, with emphasis on explaining scientific and analytical results clearly.
Statistical Computing and Data Management

Hands-on coursework focused on building reliable data workflows, writing analysis code, querying databases, and preparing messy data for modeling and reporting.

  • STATS 20 - Introduction to Statistical Programming with R: R programming for upper-division statistics, including data management, basic programming, and statistical graphics.
  • STATS 102A - Introduction to Computational Statistics with R: Numerical and computationally intensive methods, including statistical graphics, root finding, simulation, randomization testing, bootstrapping, and advanced R programming.
  • STATS 102B - Introduction to Computation and Optimization for Statistics: Vector and matrix computation, PCA, clustering, gradient-based optimization, the EM algorithm, dynamic programming, and programming for statistical problems.
  • STATS 102C - Introduction to Monte Carlo Methods: Random number generation, rejection and importance sampling, MCMC, Markov chain theory, Metropolis and Gibbs sampling, and simulation implementation.
  • STATS 404 - Statistical Computing and Programming: Statistical programming with Python and SQL, including analysis of real datasets and technical collaboration practices.
  • STATS 405 - Data Management: Reading and writing data, databases, cleaning, validation, transformation, exploratory analysis, visualization, data mining, security, ethics, and scalability.

  • STATS 133 - Introduction to Text Mining Using R: Text data concepts, text-mining tools, natural language processing, clustering, classification, and statistical modeling on textual data.
  • STATS 425 - Large Language Models in Text Mining: Text handling, natural language engineering, text-mining tools, and statistical modeling on textual data.
